# Grounded production design

Use this method when a story asks the viewer to believe a real geography, culture, era, community, institution, or work practice. It also applies to fictional stories set within a real context. Do not invoke it as a generic visual preset for a country or region, and do not burden abstract, fantastical, or purely product work with research that does not affect the result.

## Do the pass before the first image or video prompt

Inspect the supplied source and gather only the references needed to support the particular setting and action. Record a concise research-and-design block in the Production Plan and durable canon in the Project Bible:

- **Scope:** the actual geographic, cultural, temporal, institutional, and work context the story claims; use the most specific level the evidence supports.
- **Reference ledger:** each relevant reference, the exact decision it supports, its role, and what it does not prove. A reference may ground a material or work practice without controlling the next camera angle.
- **Decision labels:** mark facts visibly as **Sourced**, **Proposed**, or **Unresolved**. Sourced means the supplied or researched reference supports the claim; Proposed is a production choice; Unresolved remains unknown rather than becoming false canon.
- **Continuity consequences:** identify the facts that must survive across stills, clips, and cuts, plus the smallest acceptance criteria that make them observable.

This is prompt preparation, not a new user-approval gate. If a direct generation request is otherwise executable, complete a focused pass and continue in the same turn. If a missing fact is essential to a truthful claim or required action, hold only that affected claim or coverage; do not disguise a proposal as research.

## Carry the context into visible production decisions

Translate the ledger into concrete, shot-relevant choices instead of pasting a place label into a prompt.

| Surface | Carry forward |
| --- | --- |
| Character and wardrobe | Task-appropriate garments, construction, fit, condition, grooming, and protective items supported by the actual setting or deliberately marked as proposed. |
| Work practice and prop custody | The real task, plausible tool set, starting owner/state, reachable handoff path, contact, release, and ending owner/state. |
| Location occupancy | Who plausibly uses the place at that time, what they are doing, active work surfaces, personal effects, access paths, and the amount of activity the story needs. |
| Materials and wear | Construction, residue, repairs, weathering, handling marks, and light response that follow the setting and current action rather than a pristine generic interior. |
| Signage and artefacts | Only source-supported or explicitly proposed objects, notices, and visual language. Quote readable text only when supplied or approved; do not invent brands, logos, official notices, or documentary claims. |
| Camera blocking | The floor-map zones, action paths, prop path, sightlines, audience information, camera station, axis, foreground occlusion, and accessible view that make the action legible. |

Use one relevant reference for each job where possible: geography or material, work practice, wardrobe or prop, architecture or occupancy, and camera or composition. More images do not compensate for vague roles. Preserve a reference's limits and rights; it does not license unsupported details or factual claims.

## Keep specificity from becoming stereotype

A country, ethnicity, language, or community is not a visual style tag. Do not infer wardrobe, class, behavior, tools, cleanliness, crowding, religion, safety, or character traits from nationality alone. A flag, palette, skin tone, or decorative motif is not evidence of lived culture. Make every visual choice traceable to a relevant reference, an approved story source, or a clearly marked proposal. When the evidence is broad, keep the decision broad instead of manufacturing local detail.

## Review the result against the design

Before a still, lock, selected take, or clip is called approved, compare the generated pixels with this record and the planned shot. Check whether the visible work, occupancy, wear, prop custody, signage or artefacts, and camera blocking actually carry the intended context. Use the visual-realism gate in `$kinex-review-and-export` for physical mechanics, actual camera-angle compliance, and cross-shot continuity. A specific prompt, saved record, or completed task is not evidence that the context made it into the image.
